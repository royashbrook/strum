# a faster pitch reading (refs #1)

## evidence

The analyser window is 32768 samples, 683 ms at 48 kHz and 743 ms at 44.1 kHz. After a peg turn the FFT peak is a smear of old and new pitch for most of a second, and green needs three more frames. Simulated on a sustained tone through the detector, continuation and display memory at the real 90 ms poll, so these are synthetic detector and display-state latencies, not screen or capture timings: after a step to pitch the cents settled within 2 of truth 533 ms later and the screen turned green at 623 ms; during a peg turn at 28 cents a second the reading ran 9.6 cents behind and crossed zero 420 ms late, with the pitch already 11.8 cents past. A player turning at a natural pace overshoots before the screen says stop.

## change

Identification is untouched: which string, which harmonics, every guard. Once a string is measured, `refineHz` takes its cents from the phase advance of that tone between two adjacent segments at the end of the same time-domain buffer, which reads the last 341 ms to well under a cent. The capture loop hands the buffer it already pulls to the continuation path, and the refinement runs there so state, green and the continuation anchor all use it.

The phase advance is unambiguous only within half the segment rate of the starting guess: 2.9 Hz at 8192 samples, about 15 cents at high E, less than a turn moves in a third of a second. A quarter-tone bound on the returned value did not detect that: a high E stepped 100 cents sharp, refined from the stale in-tune peak, came back as a confident 20 to 43 cents FLAT. So three stage lengths estimate in turn, 2048, 4096 and 8192 samples, each from the last. The lengths are commensurate, so agreement alone cannot rule out a shared alias: a residual of one segment rate at 2048 wraps by one, two and four whole turns. What is checked is agreement within 10 cents together with energy at the answer of at least a third of what the FFT peak implies; while the buffer still holds two pitches the stages disagree, and a wrapped estimate lands in the window's sidelobes with a fraction of that energy. Anything else keeps the FFT reading. In the sweeps run, two rates, six strings, steps of 20 to 200 cents in both directions, three starting phases and observations every 30 ms, no accepted refinement was more than 5 cents in the wrong direction or more than 5 cents worse than the coarse estimate. That is finite coverage, not a guarantee.

Measured with the same fixtures: step within 2 cents at 263 ms instead of 533, green at 443 ms instead of 623, mean lag during the turn 4.8 cents instead of 9.6, zero crossing 240 ms late instead of 420. Steady notes read within 0.01 cents at -40 dBFS; at -75 dBFS the refinement is within a quarter cent of the FFT value. Cost is about 0.1 ms per measured string per frame on a laptop; a phone budget is not established here.

## limits

The reading is a quarter second behind a step rather than half a second, and for about 350 ms after a large step it approaches the new pitch from below by up to 8 cents before settling. Steps larger than the coarse stage can reach, about 60 cents at high E and 240 at low E, are refused until the FFT catches up, so the screen shows the old value; in the sweeps run it never showed the wrong direction. In a full strum the neighbouring strings fall inside the coarse stage's bandwidth and the refinement is mostly refused; the FFT value stands and those strings gain no speed. Polling stays at 90 ms and the three-frame confirmation, noise gates, continuation expiry and tuning-switch clearing are unchanged.

Checks keep the reported wrong-direction case as a control, sweep steps of 20 to 200 cents in both directions at three strings and two rates through the actual window, assert refusal or the right direction at every delay and the right value within half a second, pin the quarter-second settle, compare steady, faint and strummed precision with the window, cover onsets and offsets, the continuation state, and the capture wiring. Returning the FFT value fails the settle check; dropping the agreement rule fails the step sweep; dropping the energy check fails the control.
